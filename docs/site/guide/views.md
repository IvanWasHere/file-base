---
title: Views
lead: Five ways to display a folder. FileBase remembers your choice for each folder.
---

Switch views from the **view picker** in the toolbar, the **View** menu, or the keyboard:

| View | Shortcut | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Details | <kbd>⌘1</kbd> | Sorting and comparing: name, size, type, dates |
| Large Icons | <kbd>⌘2</kbd> | Spotting images and documents by their thumbnail |
| Medium Icons | <kbd>⌘3</kbd> | A balance of both |
| Small Icons | <kbd>⌘4</kbd> | Fitting lots of items on screen |
| Photos | <kbd>⌘5</kbd> | Stepping through a folder of pictures |

The view belongs to the **folder**, not the whole app. Set Pictures to Photos and Documents to Details, and each folder opens the way you left it.

## Details

![Details view with name, size, type and modified columns]({{ '/assets/img/guide/details.png' | relative_url }})

- **Click a column header** to sort by it, and click again to reverse the order.
- **Drag a header** to move the column, and drag its edge to resize it.
- Choose which columns appear in **Settings → Columns** (<kbd>⌘,</kbd>). **View → Reset Columns** puts everything back to the default.

## Icons

![Large icons view showing photo thumbnails]({{ '/assets/img/guide/icons.png' | relative_url }})

Images get real thumbnails. They're made in the background as you scroll and saved, so a folder you've visited before shows them straight away.

## Photos

![Photos view with a large image above and a filmstrip below]({{ '/assets/img/guide/photos.png' | relative_url }})

Photos view turns any folder into a simple photo browser. It shows only the images, with a large one on top and a filmstrip underneath.

- **← and →**, or the arrow buttons on either side, step through the photos.
- **Click a thumbnail** in the filmstrip to jump to it.
- Use the **zoom control** in the top-right corner (− 100% +) to look closer.

The photo on screen is the selected item, so the preview panel, the status bar and every file command act on it.

## Big folders

Every view only draws what's on screen, so a folder with tens of thousands of files scrolls as smoothly as one with ten.
